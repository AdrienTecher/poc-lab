Four ways to
wire a jam.

Same keyboard, same S-1. Four routings that trade simplicity for range — from one box on a table to your full Aira Compact chain.

01 1 box · poly Chords under, melody over

The whole jam inside one synth. Nothing to patch.

Backing
S-1 sequencer holds a low 2-note shell vamp.
You play
Live melody up high on the free voices — white keys, home on D.
Best for
Fast ambient / house sketches.
Watch
4 voices shared with the seq; one envelope serves both roles.
02 1 box · arp Hold, let it run, twist

The arpeggiator is the backing. You perform with both hands on the panel.

Backing
Hold a chord; the arp generates the moving line.
You play
Change the held shape, transpose, and ride filter / LFO / FX.
Best for
Hypnotic, knob-driven acid / psy.
Watch
The arp eats the held notes — your "melody" is shape + tweaks, not a separate line.
03 2 boxes · +beat Groove first

Drums hold the floor so the S-1 is free to lead. Sync over Aira Link.

Backing
T-8 drives the beat; S-1 seq can hold a bass.
You play
Lead or bass on the S-1 over the groove — poly trick from 01 still works.
Best for
Jams that need to drive and hold attention.
Watch
Two audio sources now — set levels; pick one box as clock.
04 3 boxes · full rig Split the roles

Harmony moves to the P-6, so the S-1 finally gets its own voice. This is the fix for "one timbre for everything."

Backing
P-6 holds the pad / chord loop; T-8 drives rhythm.
You play
Pure lead on the S-1 — its own envelope and filter, no longer shared.
Best for
The richest sound; long-form sets.
Watch
More to sync and mix; assign one box as clock master.
